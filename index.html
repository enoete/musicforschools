Rhythm

We all are familiar with the sound of rain falling ‘pitter-patter’, ‘pitter-patter’ or soldiers marching ‘left-right’, ‘left-right’. Similarly, you may have also heard the sound of the clock as it goes ‘tick-tock’, ‘tick-tock’ or listened to the sound of the pounding of yam in a mortar as the pestle goes ‘up-down’, ‘up-down’, ‘up-down’. At a distance, we hear the drummer as he beats out his music on a drum with a definite ‘boom-boom-boom’ sound. In each of these examples, what we hear is the steady movement with regular beats of ‘strong-weak’, ‘strong-weak’, or ‘strong-weak-weak’, ‘strong-weak-weak’. This steady movement is called rhythm.

In music, rhythm can be defined as the movement or flow of musical sounds. These sounds are written down by the use of symbols or notes which show their duration or length.

Simple Time Signature

A time signature consists of two figures placed at the beginning of music to indicate how many counts there are in each bar. The top figure indicates the number of beats for each bar while the lower figure shows the value of the beat. The following examples are bars having two beats and three beats respectively.

two crotchet beats
two minim beats
two quaver beats
three crotchet beats
three minim beats
three quaver beats

When the crotchet is the beat, 4 is placed as the lower figure. When the minim is the beat, 2 is the lower figure and when the quaver is the beat, 8 is the lower figure. Each of the bars will now be re-written to reflect two figures.

two crotchet beats
two minim beats
two quaver beats
three crotchet beats
three minim beats
three quaver beats

A time signature, e.g. 34 means, therefore, that there are three crotchet beats in each bar. Similarly, 32 means that there are three minim beats in each bar while 24 means that there are two crotchet beats in each bar.

Notice that when writing a time signature, no line is put between the figures as in fractions. The time signature has been described as simple because a single note - minim, crotchet, or quaver - represents one beat. A simple time signature is also one whose beat can be split into two quavers. A simple time signature has figures 2, 3 or 4 as the upper figure. Any other number, e.g. 6 or 8 as the upper figure will be wrong.

Throughout this book, it is very important that the pulse of the music is felt in all exercises that use rhythm. This is all the more important in Nigeria because all African music is extremely rhythmic. For example, a dancer can use different parts of the body to feel and express the pulse of the music. The following are different ways to show it; swaying the head, slapping the thighs and snapping the fingers. Other ways that involve using instruments apart from the drum are shaking of a tin containing pebbles or seeds or shaking a rattle made of used bottle tops attached to a wire. Musical Sounds & Pitch

The Musical Alphabet

Musical sounds are represented by notes to show not only their duration, i.e. how long or short, but also their pitch, which means how high or how low they sound. Pitch is shown by seven letters of the English Alphabet, viz A B C D E F G. These letters are used over and over again in ascending order. When one gets to the end of the musical alphabet, one starts all over again. Note that although there are many notes on the piano or any musical instrument with definite pitch, only these seven letters are used to represent all of them. For example, the lowest key on most pianos is an A. The white note immediately next to it, on its right is called B. The one next to B is called C. The other notes follow in that order of the musical alphabet. The diagram below can illustrate this.

Find the note

Notice that the black keys alternate in twos and threes, i.e. there is a group of three black keys separated by two white keys. All the other keys in between the black keys are white. If one wants to find the note C, for example, one must look for a group of two black keys. C is found to the left of them. If we want to find the key D, we must look between the two black keys while E is to the right of the two black keys.

The Octave

Observe that one C is eight notes away from another C and one D is eight notes from another D. Similarly, one F is eight notes from another F and so on. Notes of the same letter name, therefore are said to be an octave apart. Thus, if one played all the A’s they would sound somewhat alike. Similarly, all the B’s would have a distinctive B sound. An octave, therefore, means that notes are eight keys apart. A note can sound an octave higher or an octave lower.

Musical Notation

It has been indicated earlier that musical signs, which show pitch, are called notes.

When they are written on paper, they are said to be notated. However in music, five horizontal lines are used to show this notation. The five lines are called a staff or stave and they are drawn like this:

Click a line or a space.

The lines are counted from the bottom. Thus, the bottom line is counted as the first. Note that although there are five lines, there are only four spaces.

Clefs

In order to give names to notes written on the stave, a clef has to be used at the beginning of the stave. A clef can be described as the key or the clue to the kind of notes that are written on the stave or the staff. Notes that are written without an opening clef or key are without a name; they are meaningless. There are two clefs used for the keyboard (organ or piano) music. A treble clef which is also known as the G clef is used for the upper or higher notes on the keyboard while a bass clef is used for notes on the lower part.

The treble clef is also used to indicate notes to be played by the right hand when playing the piano. Similarly, the bass clef is for the left hand.
